# Visa and Entry Basics

Visa rules change often. Always confirm the current requirements with the official embassy or government website before booking.

## Common entry requirements
- A passport with enough validity (often 6 months beyond arrival) and blank pages.
- A visa, e-visa or visa-free entry depending on your nationality and the destination.
- Proof of onward or return travel.
- Proof of accommodation or an address for the stay.
- Proof of sufficient funds, which some countries may ask for.

## Schengen area (for example France, Italy, Portugal, Spain)
- The Schengen area acts as a single travel zone for short stays; many travellers need a Schengen visa for short visits, depending on their passport.
- The usual limit for short stays is 90 days within any 180-day period.
- Apply for the visa at the embassy or consulate of the country where you will spend the most time (or enter first if equal).
- Typical supporting documents: passport, photos, travel insurance, flight and hotel bookings, bank statements, proof of employment.
- Apply well in advance; appointment waits can be several weeks.

## E-visas and visas on arrival
- Some countries offer an online e-visa; apply only through the official government site, as unofficial sites often charge extra fees.
- Visa on arrival may require cash in a specific currency and passport photos.

## Customs and arrival
- Declare restricted items such as large amounts of cash, certain medicines, plants or food products.
- Check duty-free allowances for alcohol and tobacco before you travel.
- Keep your accommodation address handy; it is often requested on arrival forms.

## Tips
- Take a screenshot of visa approvals and keep a printed copy.
- Check airline rules too: airlines can deny boarding if documents are not in order.
- Allow extra time for connections that involve passport control.
