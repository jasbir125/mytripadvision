# Goa Travel Guide

## Best time to visit
- November to February: dry, pleasant and the peak season; beaches and nightlife are at their busiest and prices are highest.
- March to May: hot and humid, fewer crowds, lower prices.
- June to September: monsoon. Heavy rain, rough seas, many beach shacks closed; good for budget travel and green scenery, but swimming is often unsafe.

## North Goa vs South Goa
- North Goa (Baga, Calangute, Candolim, Anjuna, Vagator): lively, nightlife, water sports, busy beaches, markets.
- South Goa (Palolem, Agonda, Colva, Benaulim): quieter, cleaner beaches, relaxed atmosphere, fewer clubs.
- Panjim (Panaji) and Old Goa: heritage churches, Latin Quarter (Fontainhas), museums, riverside.
- Choose a base by the style of trip: party and activity in the north, rest and scenery in the south.

## Getting around
- Dabolim Airport (GOI) serves South Goa and Panjim; Manohar International Airport (GOX) in Mopa serves North Goa. Check which airport a flight uses, because transfers to the other end of the state can take 1.5–2 hours.
- Renting a scooter or motorbike is the most common way to get around; an international driving permit or valid licence and a helmet are required.
- Taxis are available but can be expensive; app-based taxis operate in limited areas. Agree on a fare or use the meter before starting.
- Distances look short on a map but traffic in the north can be slow in peak season.

## Things to do
- Beaches: Palolem and Agonda for calm, Baga and Calangute for activity.
- Heritage: Basilica of Bom Jesus, Se Cathedral, Fort Aguada, Chapora Fort.
- Fontainhas walk in Panjim for colourful Portuguese-era houses.
- Spice plantation visits, river cruises on the Mandovi, and Dudhsagar Falls (access depends on season and conditions).
- Water sports (parasailing, jet skiing) are mostly available in the north during the dry season.

## Food tips
- Try Goan fish curry rice, vindaloo, xacuti, bebinca and fresh seafood at beach shacks.
- Beach shacks are generally seasonal (open roughly October–May).

## Practical tips
- Keep valuables secure and avoid swimming in red-flag conditions or when there is no lifeguard.
- Book accommodation early for December–January.
- Carry sunscreen, a hat and insect repellent; evenings near water can be buggy.
- Alcohol rules and drinking age follow state law; check the current rules.

## Sample 3-day pacing
- Day 1: Arrive, settle in, sunset at a beach.
- Day 2: Heritage morning (Old Goa and Panjim), afternoon at a calmer beach.
- Day 3: Water sports or a scooter ride to forts and a seafood lunch, then departure.
