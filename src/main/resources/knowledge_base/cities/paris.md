# Paris Travel Guide

## Best time to visit
- Spring (April–June) and early autumn (September–October) have mild weather and long daylight.
- July–August is warm and busy; many locals leave in August and some small restaurants close.
- November–February is cold and grey, but crowds and hotel prices are lowest. Christmas markets run in December.

## Getting around
- The Metro is the fastest way to move around. Zones 1–2 cover almost every tourist site.
- Most central sights are walkable from each other (Louvre, Tuileries, Orsay, Notre-Dame, Île de la Cité).
- From Charles de Gaulle (CDG) airport, the RER B train reaches central Paris in about 35–45 minutes. Orly (ORY) connects by tram and Metro line 14.
- Taxis and ride-hailing are available but slower than the Metro in rush hour.

## Where to stay
- 1st–4th arrondissements: central, close to major sights, higher prices.
- Latin Quarter / Saint-Germain (5th–6th): lively, student area, many cafes.
- Le Marais (3rd–4th): historic, great for food and shopping.
- Montmartre (18th): quieter and scenic, but hilly; check Metro access before booking.
- Staying near a Metro station matters more than staying exactly in the centre.

## Must-see sights and timing
- Louvre: book a timed ticket in advance; arrive at opening or on a late evening opening. Plan 3+ hours.
- Eiffel Tower: book summit tickets ahead; the area is free to walk around and is best at sunset.
- Musée d'Orsay: a good, more manageable alternative to the Louvre.
- Notre-Dame: exterior and surroundings can be visited; check current access for the interior.
- Montmartre and Sacré-Cœur: go early morning to avoid crowds.
- Versailles: a half-day to full-day trip by RER C; closed on Mondays.

## Food tips
- Lunch set menus (formule/menu du jour) are cheaper than dinner.
- Bakeries (boulangeries) are an inexpensive breakfast or lunch.
- Tipping is optional; service is usually included in the bill.

## Practical tips
- Beware of pickpockets near major sights and on busy Metro lines.
- Many museums are closed on Monday or Tuesday; check the day before planning.
- A basic "Bonjour" when entering shops and restaurants is expected and appreciated.
- Carry a small amount of cash; most places accept cards.

## Sample 2-day pacing
- Day 1: Île de la Cité and the Latin Quarter in the morning, Louvre in the afternoon, Eiffel Tower at sunset.
- Day 2: Montmartre early, Le Marais for lunch, Musée d'Orsay in the afternoon, evening river walk.
